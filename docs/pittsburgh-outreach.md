# Pittsburgh engineering-leader outreach list

Prepared for Behold Labs. Compiled 2026-09-09.

## How to read this

Every name, title, address, email and phone number below was taken from a primary source —
a company's own team page, a company press release, an SEC filing, an FDA document, or a
bylined article — and each one carries the URL it came from. Nothing was inferred from a
pattern, and nothing was taken from ZoomInfo, RocketReach, Apollo or similar scrapers
without being labelled as unverified.

That has one consequence worth stating up front. **Not one email address in this document
belongs to a person you actually want to sell to.** There is exactly one published personal
address for a target (Profitmind's CTO); everything else is either a company-level inbox
like `info@` or `sales@`, or a media-relations officer at a large employer. Work emails for
engineering leaders are almost never published, and the usual way to produce them —
guessing `first.last@company.com`, or buying a scraped list — is also the usual way to get
bounces, spam-folder placement, and the occasional email to someone who left two years ago.
Section 7 covers how to close that gap properly.

What you do get: the right person, their exact current title, the company's published
contact route, and enough context to write an opening line that doesn't read like a blast.
Plus the thing a bought list can never give you — which titles in every scraped database
are simply wrong.

## 7. Turning this into deliverable contact details

Right now you have exactly two personal emails — `barney@netail.ai` (Profitmind CTO) and a
personal Yahoo address for Mach9's CTO that you should not use for business. Everything
else is a company-level channel. Four honest routes forward:

**LinkedIn, and treat it as the primary channel.** For this list it is better than email:
every person above is identified by exact current title, so a connection request with one
specific sentence about their situation (a new CPTO in month one, a raise earmarked for
engineering, a 150-person engineering org inside a grocer being acquired) will outperform
a cold email. Note that no LinkedIn URL in this document was fetched and confirmed —
LinkedIn returns HTTP 999 to automated requests — so search the name plus the company
rather than trusting a URL.

**The published company channels.** `info@thoro.ai`, `sales@lovelace.ai`,
`letschat@rimsys.io`, `support@diamondkinetics.com`, plus the contact forms for Air,
Qintel and Net Health. These are lower-status than a direct email but they are legitimate,
monitored, and at companies of 20–60 people a well-written note to `info@` genuinely
reaches the CTO.

**A licensed enrichment tool, if you want direct emails at volume.** Apollo.io, Clay,
Hunter.io or LinkedIn Sales Navigator will give you inferred addresses with confidence
scores. The important discipline: verify before sending (Hunter and Apollo both do SMTP
verification), and treat a low-confidence guess as a LinkedIn message instead. I did not
use these because inferred addresses reported as verified facts is exactly the failure mode
you asked me to avoid — but as an input to your own sending pipeline, with verification on
top, they are the normal tool for this job.

**Warm intros through the Pittsburgh ecosystem — and for the enterprises, this is the only
route that works.** Several of the startups are CMU spinouts (All Hands AI, Mach9, Thoro,
Lovelace) and Behold is CMU-adjacent, which is a real advantage over cold contact. For the
large employers, two specific venues came up repeatedly in the research and are worth
treating as the primary channel rather than a supplement:

- **Pittsburgh Technology Council** — publishes CIO of the Year winners and finalists each
  year (https://www.pghtech.org/news-and-publications/CIO26_Winners for 2026) and runs the
  awards programme those executives show up to. UPMC's CTO, PNC's business-lines CIO and
  Federated's CISO were all confirmed from that single page.
- **AI Horizons Pittsburgh 2026** (https://aihorizonspgh.com/) — BNY's Pittsburgh divisional
  CIO and BNY's global head of engineering both appear on the speaker roster.

Innovation Works and AlphaLab are the institutional routes for the startup end. The
community research pass died before I could verify which local meetups are still running in
2026, so treat the meetup layer as unfinished.

One compliance note, not legal advice: US B2B cold email is governed by CAN-SPAM, which
requires accurate header and sender information, a non-deceptive subject line, disclosure
that the message is an advertisement, a valid physical postal address, and a working
opt-out that you honour within 10 business days. Pennsylvania adds no separate B2B opt-in
regime. GDPR only becomes relevant if you email staff based in the EU. Confirm with counsel
before you scale sending.
